## [3.9.5] - 2026-09-06

### Added
- **Clock edition** (`firmware/bundles/clock/`, v0.1.0) — a panel that tells the time, and carries nothing else. Cut for a request from outside, and shelved on `/editions` under Community rather than Official: good fun, and not what Patternflow is about — Official is the maintainer's own list of what is.
- **`features/clock/`** — the hours and minutes cut out of whatever pattern is running: huge anti-aliased digits, hours over minutes on an upright panel, four across on a wide one. Inside the digits the pattern or a solid colour; outside them the pattern dimmed to taste or a solid colour; between the rows an optional bar (across, the face's own colon), cut from the pattern exactly as the digits are; the gap adjustable; the minute change crossfaded. So one layout is a cut-out, a stencil, solid digits over the pattern, or a plain clock on a coloured ground. Seven faces to choose from — Bebas Neue, Anton, Oswald, Saira Extra Condensed, Barlow Condensed, Six Caps, Squada One, all OFL — rasterised offline into 4-bit alpha cells by `toolchain/build_clock_glyphs.py`, which is also where a face of your own goes (a TTF and one line). Everything is blended into the frame on its way to the panel rather than stamped over it afterwards. `/clock` sets it up with a live preview that draws the same glyphs the panel does and sends every change as it is made — one-tap presets for the four fills, the faces as chips drawing their own digits, and the browser's own colour picker beside the swatches; `GET`/`POST /api/clock` and `GET /clock/glyphs.bin` are the contract (`docs/rest-api.md` 1.3). The time zone is a POSIX TZ string with its DST rule — the weather page's UTC offset cannot say when summer time starts — and the clock owns it: whatever else sets `TZ` is put back within a second. On first boot the offset a panel had already given the weather page is read, never written, as the starting zone. Nothing is drawn until NTP has answered once. Orientation counts quarter turns from the wide way; 1, upright, is the default.
- Core: `PatternflowClock::beginSyncTz()` and `assertTz()` in `src/core_clock.h`, the POSIX-zone entry points beside the minutes-offset one; and **`composeFrame`**, a new hook at the tail of `PFFeature` — the finished canvas on its way to the panel, before the blit, so a feature can blend with real alpha or mask the whole frame. `PFCanvas::present()` asks one function pointer, the sketch wires it to the dispatcher, and the canvas itself is never written (patterns that accumulate across frames own it).
- The tooling knows the fourth composition: `build.sh all` builds and scans `clock` (the feature's marker is `[CLOCK] /clock ready`), `release.py cut --clock vX`, `check_versions.py`, the console page list and the fake device (`/api/clock`, `/clock/glyphs.bin`).

### Added — panel
- **Browsing with K4 no longer loads every pattern it passes.** A module's cost on the way in is its own `setup()` — 10 ms for a light one, 2.3 s for *Branched flow*, 2.9 s for *Two-stream* — and SELECT mode paid it on every detent: the loop stood still for that long while the encoder kept counting, and the accumulated detents landed all at once when it came back, a panel that froze for two seconds and then jumped five patterns. The knob now moves only the highlight, three detents per pattern (one per detent overshot); the pattern loads once the knob has rested for a third of a second, and until then the panel shows the pattern's **thumbnail** — the 128×64 frame it last drew, taken off the canvas when a pattern is left after running for two seconds, kept in PSRAM and written beside the module as `/patterns/<slug>.thumb` (16 KB each; `src/core_thumbs.h`). Captured before gamma and white balance and painted back through the same `present()`, so the picture is what the pattern looked like. A pattern nobody has played yet shows only its name until it has; the first slow pass through the list builds the set. Deleting a module deletes its picture, formatting the volume forgets them all, and `GET /api/patterns/file?ext=thumb` hands one to a console or a site. The load itself runs on a task on Core 0 (`activatePatternAsync`): the frame never stops and the knob keeps answering while a heavy `setup()` runs, the panel shows the thumbnail until the pattern is ready, and where the knob rests last is what loads next. The same path serves switches from the console, OSC, MIDI and a show; `/api/status` says `loading` while one is on its way, and uploads and deletes wait for a load in flight before evicting.

### Fixed — panel
- **The UPDATE card no longer tears against the pattern while an image is received.** Since the upload handler moved to the network core (3.9.1) its progress callback kept drawing the card and flipping the panel from there, while `loop()` went on drawing the pattern — two tasks flipping one DMA buffer for the length of the upload. The callback now only reports the percentage; `loop()` shows the card, as it does every other one.
- **The name that appears when a pattern is chosen from the console reads upright.** It was drawn along the panel's long axis — sideways on a panel stood on its end — and is now portrait like the SELECT screen, wrapped the same way for long names.

### Fixed — core
- **The console survives a slow Wi-Fi link.** A panel whose link was moving 2–5 KB/s (round trips of ~0.4 s with retransmissions; a healthy panel on the same network does 3–60 ms) got every console page cut short: the page sender's hard 5-second budget — written for the days when a send ran inside `loop()` and stalled the render — broke off with the header already promising the full `Content-Length`, so `/` arrived as 10–25 KB of 26 KB and the 63 KB audio page never at all, and the browser showed a page whose script ends mid-word. Since 3.9.1 the sender runs on the network core and the render never sees it, so the budget was only ever hurting. It now gives up only when the peer stops accepting bytes for ~20 s (or after a two-minute ceiling), and when it does it closes the connection instead of leaving a short body looking complete; on the single-core fallback the 5 s cap stays. Measured with a client holding a 1 KB window: 17.5 KB delivered whole in 22 s where it used to be cut at 5; a client that reads nothing is dropped at 20 s and the next one is served in 30 ms.
- **Console pages are sent gzip-compressed** — `console_pages.py build` lays a gzip twin under every page literal (and under `pf-console.js` and the unzip library), the handlers send that with `Content-Encoding: gzip`, and `check` proves each twin still decompresses to its page in CI. A third of the bytes cross the air (26.5 → 8.6 KB for `/`, 63 → 17.6 KB for `/audio-in`), which on a bad link is a third of the time; the raw literals stay in the headers for the tooling and drop out of the image, which shrinks by ~140 KB.
- **The network core no longer reboots on a request that stalls.** The vendored `WebServer` read request lines, headers and raw upload bodies through Arduino's `Stream::timedRead()`, a spin loop that never yields. Harmless while the server ran on Core 1; since 3.9.1 it runs on Core 0, whose idle task feeds a 5-second watchdog that panics — so a request that paused for five seconds mid-header, or a `.pfm` upload trickling in over a slow link, took the whole board down. Reproduced on a bench panel (`Task watchdog got triggered: IDLE0`, reset, back in ten seconds) and gone after: the parser now waits with `delay(1)` between bytes, bounded by the same timeouts as before (`src/webserver/VENDORED.md`, fix 2).
- **A big pattern can no longer take the console down with it.** The module loader placed a module's data sections in internal RAM whenever they fit, and internal RAM is what Wi-Fi, lwIP and every HTTP connection are made of. A community pattern whose tables came to 46 KB left 2.7 KB free after boot: the panel drew on, every connection died at the SYN, and nothing anywhere said why. Data sections over 16 KB, or any that would leave less than 24 KB of internal heap, now go to PSRAM first (`PF_MODULE_DATA_INTERNAL_MAX`, `PF_MODULE_INTERNAL_RESERVE`); the same pattern now leaves 42 KB free with 3.9 KB of it internal, and the console answers in 50 ms while it plays.

### Changed — features
- **MIDI and OSC report hands, not lanes, and MIDI knob output is paced.** On the Audio edition the microphone moves the knobs through lanes, and by the time the frame reached the MIDI and OSC features that motion looked like an encoder — so a quiet room's noise floor went out as 26–43 control changes a second (two datagrams per knob per frame over OSC, once a host is known), filled the Wi-Fi driver's eight transmit buffers, and starved the console's own traffic behind a wall of `could not send data: 12` on the serial log. Both features now skip a knob a lane is moving (`PF_MIDI_OUT_LANE_MOTION` / `PF_OSC_OUT_LANE_MOTION` restore the old behaviour), and MIDI sends at most one CC per knob per 50 ms, trailing-edge, so the last value always lands (`docs/midi-spec.md` 1.1, `docs/osc-spec.md` 1.1).

### Added — API
- **A brightness slider on the console home page**, under the panel switch — the value K1's long-press session sets, written over `GET /api/display?brightness=5..255` (applied through the power clamp on the next frame, saved to NVS once it has been still for 3 s) and reported in `/api/status` as `brightness`, so the slider follows the knob and the knob follows the slider.
- `GET /api/status` gains `resetReason` (`"poweron"`, `"sw"`, `"panic"`, `"task_wdt"`, `"brownout"`…) next to `uptime` — two reboots went unnoticed in one afternoon because from the network they look exactly like a power cut — and `load.internal` / `load.psram`, where the resident module's sections landed. The same reset reason is printed once at boot. `docs/rest-api.md` 1.4.
